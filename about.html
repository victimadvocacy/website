Read Ally’s Full Biography

Ally works directly with litigation teams on behalf of people who have been financially exploited, including trust, estate and fiduciary disputes and elder financial abuse, building a clearer factual foundation for counsel. As founder of Victim Advocacy Associates, she takes on the detailed work of reconstructing events, organizing financial records, developing evidence chronologies and coordinating client communication. Her work gives counsel organized material to assess, focused questions to pursue and a more coherent understanding of the matter.

Her value lies in connecting information across records and client accounts. She examines how dates, transactions and communications fit together, identifies inconsistencies for counsel’s review and helps clients tell their story clearly, in their own words. This gives attorneys a practical resource for working through fragmented information while keeping legal evaluation and strategy with counsel.

Ally’s background directly supports this work. She earned a B.A. in Organizational Communication, with a minor in Marketing, from Pepperdine University, grounding her approach in how information is gathered, structured and communicated. Her experience as a peer mentor and in case management in mental health settings informs her ability to listen carefully, approach sensitive conversations and maintain productive client relationships, and she is trained in crisis management and trauma-informed communication. She is pursuing a Master of Arts in Clinical Mental Health Counseling at Chadron State College, in a CACREP-accredited program she expects to complete in spring 2027, on the path to licensure as a Licensed Professional Counselor. She also speaks Spanish at a workplace-proficient level.

Ally came to this work after seeing financial exploitation up close. What stayed with her was how alone the people it touches are: nobody talks about it, and the shame keeps people quiet. Most don’t know what to do first or who to call. VAA exists so the next person does.

Ally is a counseling student, not a licensed counselor. VAA’s support is nonclinical advocacy: Victim Advocacy Associates does not provide legal advice, legal representation, counseling, therapy or mental health treatment.

Independent Professionals We Work With

When a matter calls for licensed work, VAA can introduce independent professionals we know well, including Bruce Bealke, a trial lawyer with 40 years of experience, and a licensed private investigator who is a retired police captain with 25 years in law enforcement. Each works independently, under their own engagement and license. VAA does not share fees with them or direct their work.

Professional services are separately scoped. VAA’s advocacy complements the client’s legal team and does not replace legal representation or clinical care.

Experience with Attorneys and Complex Records

Our work includes trust, estate and fiduciary disputes, elder and financial abuse, and civil fraud, along with other matters where someone has been harmed. Source-referenced chronologies, evidence tables, documented-loss summaries and factual memoranda give counsel an organized record to evaluate.

How We Work With Clients

Our founder’s background in mental health peer support and graduate counseling study informs how we approach sensitive conversations and client coordination. We start from the belief that clients are the experts in their own lives and that recovery after harm is rarely a straight line, so support is collaborative and paced to the person. VAA helps clients feel supported throughout the legal process their firm manages and the transition afterward. We follow the firm’s communication preferences and keep legal questions with its team. VAA does not provide counseling, therapy or mental health treatment.

Clear Commitments and an Honest Factual Record

We listen carefully and distinguish reported accounts from what the records establish. Missing information and conflicting evidence stay visible. Deliverables, timing and communication are defined in writing; changes are discussed before proceeding.
