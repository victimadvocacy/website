Read Ally’s Full Biography

Ally helps attorneys build a clearer factual foundation for matters involving fraud, financial abuse, fiduciary misconduct and other harm. As founder of Victim Advocacy Associates, she takes on the detailed work of reconstructing events, organizing financial records, developing evidence chronologies and coordinating client communication. Her work gives counsel organized material to assess, focused questions to pursue and a more coherent understanding of the matter. She founded VAA in 2026 to build this work into a business, alongside her continuing work as an independent consultant under a services agreement effective April 2026.

Her value lies in connecting information across records and client accounts. She examines how dates, transactions and communications fit together, identifies inconsistencies for counsel’s review and helps clients tell their story clearly, in their own words. This gives attorneys a practical resource for working through fragmented information while keeping legal evaluation and strategy with counsel.

Ally’s background directly supports this work. She earned a B.A. in Organizational Communication, with a minor in Marketing, from Pepperdine University, grounding her approach in how information is gathered, structured and communicated. Her experience as a peer mentor and in case management in mental health settings informs her ability to listen carefully, approach sensitive conversations and maintain productive client relationships, and she is trained in crisis management and trauma-informed communication. She is pursuing a Master of Arts in Clinical Mental Health Counseling at Chadron State College, in a CACREP-accredited program, with a 4.0 GPA. She is expected to graduate in December 2026, completing graduate training toward licensure as a Licensed Professional Counselor. She also speaks Spanish at a workplace-proficient level.

Ally came to this work after seeing fraud up close. What stayed with her was how alone the people it touches are: nobody talks about fraud, and the shame keeps people quiet. Most don’t know what to do first or who to call. VAA exists so the next person does.

Ally is a counseling student, not a licensed counselor. VAA’s support is nonclinical advocacy: Victim Advocacy Associates does not provide legal advice, legal representation, counseling, therapy or mental health treatment.

Our Advocate Standard

Every Advocate Must Clear This Bar

Your lawyers stay on the legal work. Before an advocate works a firm’s matter, that advocate must complete every item below. This is VAA’s required process. It is not a certification that has been granted.

  • Trained to the NACP Standard. Every advocate must be trained to the NACP standard, aligned with NOVA’s National Advocate Credentialing Program, including at least 40 hours of victim advocacy training. This is not a claim that anyone holds NACP.
  • Criminal Background Check. Every advocate must complete a criminal background check before working a firm’s matter.
  • Confidentiality and Conflict Checks. Every advocate must sign confidentiality and conflict-check agreements before the file is opened.
  • Trauma-Informed and Court-Accompaniment Training. Every advocate must complete trauma-informed training and court-accompaniment training.
  • Supervised Work Before Independent Work. Every advocate must complete supervised work on files before working a firm’s matter independently.
  • Ongoing Continuing Education. Every advocate must continue that education after beginning independent work.

Your firm keeps the legal work. We do not replace legal representation, and we do not provide counseling or therapy.

Experience with Attorneys and Complex Records

Ally’s recent work as an independent consultant, under a services agreement effective April 2026, covers seven matters and projects: three civil-litigation matters in California and Florida, a fraud matter’s records and victim-advocacy file, a bank-related records research file, a client-support matter and a postconviction records project.

That work has included chronologies built from court records, financial documents and correspondence; evidence indexes, case maps and relationship maps; reviewing records for gaps and inconsistencies for counsel; financial and business-record review to document transactions and potential damages; discovery support, such as document requests, subpoena support and identifying witnesses and records custodians; and following up on outstanding records with clients, victims and witnesses. Some assignments ran for months across several stages; others were single projects, such as organizing a historical record. These are recent matters, not a full career count. Matter names stay confidential; firms can ask for more detail, which we share where counsel permits.

How We Work with Clients

Our founder’s background in mental health peer support and graduate counseling study informs how we approach sensitive conversations and client coordination. We start from the belief that clients are the experts in their own lives and that recovery after harm is rarely a straight line, so support is collaborative and paced to the person. VAA helps clients feel supported throughout the legal process their firm manages and the transition afterward. We follow the firm’s communication preferences and keep legal questions with its team. VAA does not provide counseling, therapy or mental health treatment.

Clear Commitments and an Honest Factual Record

We listen carefully and distinguish reported accounts from what the records establish. Missing information and conflicting evidence stay visible. Deliverables, timing and communication are defined in writing; changes are discussed before proceeding.
