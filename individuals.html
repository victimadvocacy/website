Practical Support That Follows Through

Support can include attending hearings, preparing for meetings, drafting your victim impact statement with you, connecting you with licensed professional counselors (LPCs) or community resources, coordinating appointments and checking in after difficult milestones.

Help Gathering and Organizing Evidence

We help you track down and request the records that support your case: bank and account statements, messages, receipts, police reports and court records. We show you how to save them so nothing is lost, then organize an agreed set of materials and prepare factual summaries for review. Records work is available when needed; it is not a prerequisite for advocacy.

Your Victim Impact Statement,
in Your Words

A victim impact statement is your chance to tell the court how the harm has affected your life. Finding the words can be the hardest part, and you shouldn’t have to face a blank page alone. We make the writing easier: you tell us what you want the court to know, and we help you put it in order, in your own words, at your own pace.

  1. We listen. A trauma-informed conversation about how the harm has affected your money, health, safety, family and work. You decide what to share and when.
  2. We help you shape it. We organize what you’ve told us into a clear draft, in your own words.
  3. You revise. You change anything until it sounds like you. Nothing stays in that you don’t agree with.
  4. You approve and sign. The statement is yours, and you are its author.
  5. We help you deliver it. We coordinate with the prosecutor’s victim assistance coordinator on format and deadlines, practice with you, and can sit with you in court if you read it aloud.

Your statement is about your experience. If it mentions dates or dollar amounts, we help you check them against your records. Tell us your sentencing or hearing date when you first contact us, so we can plan around it. Requests about sentencing or restitution go through the prosecutor or your attorney; VAA does not give legal advice. VAA does not provide counseling or therapy.

What Can I Expect?
  1. We listen. Tell us what happened in your own words, at your own pace.
  2. You know the cost first. Services, fees and duration are agreed in writing.
  3. We support and organize. We provide the agreed advocacy or review the agreed records. We explain the record-sharing process before you send materials.
  4. We help with the next step. For records work, you receive the agreed factual summary or file. With your authorization, we coordinate delivery to your attorney. Continued advocacy is separately scoped.
Don’t Have an Attorney Yet?

We can introduce you to attorneys in our network who handle matters like yours, to licensed professional counselors (LPCs) if you would like emotional support, and to other services your situation calls for. We vet everyone in our network before we refer you. You decide whether to contact them, any engagement is directly between you and them, and VAA never pays or receives referral fees.

Already Working with an Attorney?

With your authorization, we coordinate with your attorney and follow the agreed communication plan. Keep contacting your legal team directly for legal advice, case updates and strategy; VAA adds personal and practical support throughout the process.

I Already Have a Free Victim Advocate. Do I Need You?

Maybe not. Advocates through a district attorney’s office, a shelter or a victim services program are free, and they may be all you need, so start there. Private support can add help gathering and organizing your records, one steady person across criminal and civil matters, and support after the case closes. If you work with us, we can work alongside your advocate.

Will My Calls Be Recorded or Shared?

We don’t record our conversations with you unless your attorney asks us to, and we’d tell you and ask your permission first. When you work with us through your attorney, we share updates with your legal team as agreed in writing. If you come to us on your own, we keep what you tell us confidential except where the law requires otherwise. Conversations with VAA are not protected the way conversations with your lawyer are. In practice, that means the other side in a legal matter could, in some cases, ask a court for what you’ve told us. If we ever receive a subpoena or other request about you, we will tell you, and your attorney if you have one, promptly where the law allows. If you’re unsure whether to share something, ask your attorney first.

What Happens After the Matter Ends?

Your need for support may continue. An agreed transition plan can include resource coordination, referral follow-up and practical next steps, within a defined scope and duration.

What Will It Cost?

The first 15-minute call is free. A records assessment is a fixed $500, and a victim impact statement is a flat $450. Comprehensive case work typically runs $1,500–$6,000, depending on the size of the record (larger matters are quoted in stages), and ongoing support during an active matter is $2,500 a month, including up to 20 hours. You receive the price in writing before any paid work begins, and fees are never tied to the outcome of your case.

A Word About Recovery Scams

Be cautious of anyone promising to recover lost money for an upfront fee. We don’t recover money, and we won’t promise to. We never ask for gift cards, cryptocurrency or wire transfers. If someone says they’re from VAA and asks for money before you’ve signed a written agreement, it isn’t us.

You can report suspected fraud to the FBI’s IC3 ↗ or the Federal Trade Commission ↗.

What Is Outside VAA's Role?

VAA does not provide legal advice, representation, counseling, therapy or licensed private investigation, and does not guarantee a case outcome. Counseling connections mean helping you reach an appropriate provider, not providing treatment.
