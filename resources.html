Where to Start After
Fraud or Abuse

If you don’t know what to do first, that’s normal. These are the first steps, and the official places to go that are free to everyone.

If You Are in Danger

Get Safe First

  • 911 If you are in immediate danger.
  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, by call or text, at any hour.
  • 800-799-7233 National Domestic Violence Hotline, at any hour. Or text START to 88788, or chat at thehotline.org ↗
  • 800-656-4673 RAINN National Sexual Assault Hotline, at any hour.
  • 844-762-8483 StrongHearts Native Helpline, for American Indians and Alaska Natives affected by domestic, dating or sexual violence. Call, text or chat at strongheartshelpline.org ↗
  • LOVEIS to 22522 love is respect, for teens and young adults in an abusive relationship, at any hour. Or call 866-331-9474.
  • HOME to 741741 Crisis Text Line, free support by text at any hour.

After Fraud

Your First Seven Steps

01

Stop Sending Money

Even if you’re promised it will be returned. Don’t warn or confront the person.

02

Save Everything

Messages, emails, statements, receipts and screenshots, with dates. Don’t delete anything, even if it’s embarrassing.

03

Call Your Bank

Use the number on your card or statement. Ask them to stop or reverse payments and secure your accounts.

04

Freeze Your Credit

A freeze is free at each of the three credit bureaus and stops new accounts being opened in your name.

05

Report It

To the FBI, the FTC and your local police. If the person harmed is an older adult or has a disability, also call Adult Protective Services. Ask for a report number and keep it.

06

Write a Timeline

While it’s fresh: what happened, when, how much, and who was involved. It doesn’t need to be perfect.

07

Get Support

Talk to someone you trust, a counselor or an advocate. You don’t have to do this by yourself.

Where to Report

Official Places to Report

Online Fraud

FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center

For scams and fraud that happened online, by text or by phone.

ic3.gov ↗
Scams and Fraud

Federal Trade Commission

Reports help the FTC spot patterns and bring cases against scammers.

reportfraud.ftc.gov ↗
Identity Theft

IdentityTheft.gov

If your name, Social Security number or accounts were used. You get a personal recovery plan.

identitytheft.gov ↗
Age 60 and Older

National Elder Fraud Hotline

833-372-8311 (833-FRAUD-11). Free help from the U.S. Department of Justice, Monday through Friday, 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Eastern, in English and Spanish.

justice.gov/stopelderfraud ↗
Texas Only

Texas Abuse Hotline

800-252-5400, any hour. Report abuse, neglect or financial exploitation of an adult 65 or older or an adult with a disability. For a nursing home, assisted living facility or other licensed care provider, call HHSC Complaint and Incident Intake, 800-458-9858. In another state, see Adult Protective Services.

txabusehotline.org ↗
Local

Your Local Police

Use the non-emergency number. A police report is often required by banks, insurers and compensation programs.

More Free Help in Every State

Crime Victim Compensation

Your State’s Compensation Program

Every state has a program that may help with costs such as counseling, lost wages and relocation after a crime. These programs usually don’t repay money that was stolen. The national association links to each one.

nacvcb.org ↗
Abuse of Older Adults or People with Disabilities

Adult Protective Services

Report abuse, neglect or financial exploitation to the office in the person’s state. For other local help for older adults, call the Eldercare Locator at 800-677-1116, weekdays.

napsa-now.org ↗

877-908-3360, weekdays 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern. Free for anyone, any age; you don’t need to be an AARP member.

If a bank, lender or other financial company won’t help after fraud, file a complaint online or call 855-411-2372.

SEC and FINRA

Report suspected investment fraud to the SEC. Investors 60 and older can also call the FINRA Securities Helpline for Seniors at 844-574-3577, weekdays.

sec.gov/tcr ↗
Mail Fraud

U.S. Postal Inspection Service

877-876-2455, or report online. The Postal Inspection Service does not call people from this number, so treat any such call as a scam.

uspis.gov ↗

Stalking and Safety

If Someone Is Following or Watching You

  • Stalking Information, safety planning and a log to record incidents, from SPARC ↗
  • Tech Safety How to check phones, accounts and devices for tracking, from NNEDV Safety Net ↗
  • Your Records Keep a dated log, save messages and keep camera footage that can be exported. Evidence helps if you seek a protective order.
A Word About Recovery Scams. People who have lost money are often contacted again by someone promising to get it back for an upfront fee. Real agencies never charge to recover your money. Never pay with gift cards, cryptocurrency or a wire to an individual. Read the FTC’s Warning ↗
