Our Advocate Standard

Every Advocate Must Clear This Bar

Your lawyers stay on the legal work. Before an advocate works a firm’s matter, that advocate must complete every item below. This is VAA’s required process.

  • 40 Hours of Victim Advocacy Training. VAA requires at least 40 hours of victim advocacy training before an advocate works on a firm’s matter.
  • Criminal Background Check. Every advocate must complete a criminal background check before working a firm’s matter.
  • Confidentiality and Conflict Checks. Every advocate must sign confidentiality and conflict-check agreements before the file is opened.
  • Trauma-Informed and Court-Accompaniment Training. Every advocate must complete trauma-informed training and court-accompaniment training.
  • Supervised Work Before Independent Work. Every advocate must complete supervised work on files before working a firm’s matter independently.
  • Ongoing Continuing Education. Every advocate must continue that education after beginning independent work.

Your firm keeps the legal work. We do not replace legal representation, and we do not provide counseling or therapy.

How Does VAA Work with Our Team?

Start with a defined project, a pilot matter or ongoing support. We agree on services, fees, authorized client contact and a reporting plan in writing. VAA follows your communication preferences; clients continue to contact your team directly for legal advice, case updates and strategy. We work with firms in every state, by phone and video, and travel for hearings when a matter needs it. If you prefer, we work only from records your team provides, with no direct contact with your client.

Could VAA Be Called as a Witness?

Possibly. Anyone who gathers or organizes records in a matter can be asked to testify or produce materials. We prepare our work so it can stand up to that: every entry is tied to its source, and our methods are documented. Engaging VAA at counsel’s direction, and labeling work product accordingly, gives your team the strongest position to evaluate protection. Whether that protection applies is counsel’s call. If we receive a subpoena or other request about a matter, we notify the engaging firm promptly, where the law permits.

How Do You Protect Client Records?

We agree on how records will be shared before any are sent: your firm’s approved platform, or a matter folder open only to agreed participants. We never ask for sensitive records by ordinary email or through our contact form. Access is limited to the people working on the matter. If your firm has its own data-handling requirements, we follow them. At the end of an engagement we return or destroy records as your firm directs and confirm in writing.

Do You Follow Our Firm’s Confidentiality Rules?

Yes, with one exception. We follow your firm’s confidentiality and supervision instructions and will sign your nondisclosure agreement. If the law requires us to report suspected abuse or exploitation of an older or disabled adult, we will report it right away and tell your firm promptly, where the law allows. We run a conflicts check before taking any matter and won’t work for anyone on the other side of the same or a closely related matter.

How Do You Keep Our Team Informed?

Before work begins, we agree in writing on what we report, to whom and how often. By default we provide short written updates covering practical support, upcoming dates and anything that should go to your team. If you prefer, we update you by phone instead and follow your instructions on what we write down after client calls. We don’t record client conversations unless your firm directs it in writing, because a verbatim record of a client’s account can raise privilege and discovery questions that counsel should decide. When a firm does request recordings, we record only with the client’s consent and follow the firm’s instructions on storage, transcription and access.

Can You Help Us Find an Expert Witness?

Yes. We can introduce your team to expert witnesses we know. Your team evaluates their qualifications. Experts are retained by and work directly with counsel under their own terms, and VAA does not pay or accept referral fees.

How Are Fees and Professional Roles Handled?

VAA provides paid, nonlegal services. Scope, fees, payer, permissions and duration are agreed before work begins. Fees are not contingent on a recovery, and VAA does not pay or accept referral fees. Licensed legal, clinical and investigative work belongs to appropriately licensed professionals.

VAA's involvement does not itself establish attorney-client privilege or work-product protection; counsel evaluates those issues for the matter.
