What Every VAA Advocate Completes

Today, VAA’s advocate is its founder, Ally Arpey. Anyone who joins completes every step below before working a firm’s matter.

We do not give legal advice, and we do not provide counseling or therapy.

The checklist

  • Victim advocacy training. At least 40 hours, following the training standard of NOVA’s National Advocate Credentialing Program (NACP). Ally Arpey has completed NACP training.
  • Background check. Completed before an advocate works a firm’s matter.
  • Signed agreements. Confidentiality and conflict checks before the file is opened.
  • Trauma-informed and court training. Completed before independent work.
  • Supervised files. Reviewed by the founder before an advocate works a matter alone.
  • Continuing education. Ongoing after independent work begins.

How We Work

Our work is nonclinical advocacy, not counseling, therapy, or mental health treatment.

Independent Professionals We Work With

When a matter calls for licensed work, VAA can introduce independent professionals we know well. Each works independently, under their own engagement and license. VAA does not share fees with them or direct their work.

Professional services are separately scoped. VAA’s advocacy complements the client’s legal team and does not replace legal representation or clinical care.

How We Work with Clients

Our founder’s background in mental health peer support informs how we approach sensitive conversations and client coordination. We start from the belief that clients are the experts in their own lives and that recovery after harm is rarely a straight line, so support is collaborative and paced to the person. VAA helps clients feel supported throughout the legal process their firm manages and the transition afterward. We follow the firm’s communication preferences and keep legal questions with its team. VAA does not provide counseling, therapy or mental health treatment.

Clear Commitments and an Honest Factual Record

We listen carefully and distinguish reported accounts from what the records establish. Missing information and conflicting evidence stay visible. Deliverables, timing and communication are defined in writing; changes are discussed before proceeding.

What the pilot includes

Assumptions

  • Counsel sends a defined set of records (statements, correspondence, court filings) and one firm contact.
  • Scope, hours cap and deliverables are agreed in writing before work begins.
  • VAA works only from what counsel provides and what the client reports, with no database searches and no licensed investigation.

What the firm gets

  • A chronology with a source cite on every entry, keeping what the records show separate from what was reported.
  • An evidence index for the records in scope.
  • A list of missing records and open questions for counsel to decide on.
  • A summary after client calls, with legal questions passed to counsel.

Timing

These are targets, not guarantees.

  • Kickoff call usually within 2 business days of signing.
  • First draft usually within 10 business days of receiving the records.
  • One round of revisions after counsel reviews.

Review steps

  • Counsel reviews the draft and flags changes.
  • VAA revises and delivers the final.
  • A short wrap-up call on continuing monthly or closing out.

The Details, When You Need Them

Our Services

We organize records, build source-referenced chronologies and evidence indexes, and summarize documented transactions for counsel’s review.

We help your client organize what happened, prepare for meetings with your team, and keep track of records and next steps. We coordinate with your firm under the engagement.

VAA does not perform licensed private investigation. Tasks requiring a license are handled by appropriately licensed professionals under a separate, agreed scope. We do not provide legal representation or legal advice.

What You Receive

  • Chronology. Relevant events from the reviewed materials, with source references and unresolved dates identified.
  • Evidence index. Records within the agreed scope, catalogued and labeled so counsel can locate them.
  • Documented-loss summary. Amounts and transactions supported by the records, with estimates and gaps clearly identified.
  • Factual memo. A concise account of what the reviewed records show, what is reported and what remains unresolved.

Scope, Fees & Professional Boundaries

  • No guaranteed outcome. We cannot promise recovery, attorney acceptance or a particular result.
  • No legal advice or representation. VAA organizes information for review; legal decisions belong to a licensed attorney.
  • No unlicensed professional services. Work requiring a license is handled by appropriately licensed professionals.
  • Written scope. Any additional work requires written agreement before proceeding. See pricing.
