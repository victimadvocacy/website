Our Advocate Standard

Every Advocate Must Clear This Bar

Your lawyers stay on the legal work. Before an advocate works a firm’s matter, that advocate must complete every item below. This is VAA’s required process.

  • 40 Hours of Victim Advocacy Training.VAA requires at least 40 hours of victim advocacy training before an advocate works on a firm’s matter.
  • Criminal Background Check. Every advocate must complete a criminal background check before working a firm’s matter.
  • Confidentiality and Conflict Checks. Every advocate must sign confidentiality and conflict-check agreements before the file is opened.
  • Trauma-Informed and Court-Accompaniment Training. Every advocate must complete trauma-informed training and court-accompaniment training.
  • Supervised Work Before Independent Work. Every advocate must complete supervised work on files before working a firm’s matter independently.
  • Ongoing Continuing Education. Every advocate must continue that education after beginning independent work.

These are VAA’s internal requirements, not an external credential.

Your firm keeps the legal work. We do not replace legal representation, and we do not provide counseling or therapy.

Experience with Attorneys and Complex Records

Ally’s recent work as an independent consultant, under a services agreement effective April 2026, covers seven matters and projects: three civil-litigation matters in California and Florida, a fraud matter’s records and victim-advocacy file, a bank-related records research file, a client-support matter and a postconviction records project.

That work has included chronologies built from court records, financial documents and correspondence; evidence indexes, case maps and relationship maps; reviewing records for gaps and inconsistencies for counsel; financial and business-record review to document transactions and potential damages; discovery support, such as document requests, subpoena support and identifying witnesses and records custodians; and following up on outstanding records with clients, victims and witnesses. Some assignments ran for months across several stages; others were single projects, such as organizing a historical record. These are recent matters, not a full career count. Matter names stay confidential; firms can ask for more detail, which we share where counsel permits.

How We Work with Clients

Our founder’s graduate study in clinical mental health counseling informs a trauma-informed approach to advocacy and client support under the firm’s direction. It shapes how we listen, handle difficult conversations, and stay with clients through a legal matter. This is nonclinical advocacy, not counseling, therapy, or mental health treatment. We start from the belief that clients are the experts in their own lives and that recovery after harm is rarely a straight line, so support is collaborative and paced to the person. VAA helps clients with records, practical hearing preparation, and follow-through throughout the legal process their firm manages and the transition afterward. We follow the firm’s communication preferences and keep legal questions with its team. VAA does not provide counseling, therapy or mental health treatment.

Clear Commitments and an Honest Factual Record

We listen carefully and distinguish reported accounts from what the records establish. Missing information and conflicting evidence stay visible. Deliverables, timing and communication are defined in writing; changes are discussed before proceeding.

The Details, When You Need Them

Our Services

We organize records, build source-referenced chronologies and evidence indexes, and summarize documented transactions for counsel's review.

VAA does not perform licensed private investigation. Tasks requiring a license are handled by appropriately licensed professionals under a separate, agreed scope.

We help your client organize what happened, prepare for meetings with your team, and keep track of records and next steps. We coordinate with your firm under the engagement.

We do not provide legal representation or legal advice.

What You Receive

• Chronology. Relevant events from the reviewed materials, with source references and unresolved dates identified.

• Evidence index. Records within the agreed scope, catalogued and labeled so counsel can locate them.

• Documented-loss summary. Amounts and transactions supported by the records, with estimates and gaps clearly identified.

• Factual memo. A concise account of what the reviewed records show, what is reported and what remains unresolved.

Scope, Fees & Professional Boundaries

• No guaranteed outcome. We cannot promise recovery, attorney acceptance or a particular result.

• No legal advice or representation. VAA organizes information for review; legal decisions belong to a licensed attorney.

• No unlicensed professional services. Work requiring a license is handled by appropriately licensed professionals.

• Written fees and scope. Any additional work or charges require written agreement before proceeding.
