Long-Running Fraud · 2026

A Years-Long Fraud, Organized for Law Enforcement

A family had lost a significant sum over several years to someone they trusted, through one invented crisis after another. They didn’t know who to call.

  • A source-referenced file built from public court records in several jurisdictions, with every claim tied to its source.
  • Help reporting to the appropriate local and federal agencies, with the records each one asked for.
  • Immediate protective steps, including a credit freeze after the family’s identity was misused.
  • Records shared with authorities on request, organized and indexed.

Details are changed or left out to protect the people involved. The matter remains with law enforcement, and we don’t claim any charge, conviction or recovery resulted from this work.

Multi-Year Dispute · 2026

Years of Records, One Timeline Counsel Could Use

The facts were spread across financial records, communications and filings in more than one related case.

  • One master chronology replaced digging through separate case files.
  • Any event could be traced straight to its source document.
  • Each related proceeding could be followed on its own.
  • Open questions and unreviewed records were flagged for the legal team.

Preparation work. We don’t claim it caused any ruling, settlement or recovery.

Financial-Record Review · 2026

An Agency’s Ledger, Checked Line by Line

The main record of what a performer was paid was the agency’s own ledger, and it didn’t match up cleanly with the other records.

  • Counsel could see which payments the records confirmed and which they didn’t.
  • Possible duplicates and charges outside the contract terms were flagged.
  • Amounts the agency reported were kept apart from bank-confirmed amounts.
  • Counsel got a specific list of records to request next.

Unmatched entries aren’t proof of theft or a final loss figure on their own, and no recovery has been established.

Client Records Packet · 2026

A Client Ready for Counsel’s Review

A client wanted an attorney to look at older criminal records but had only part of the paperwork.

  • The attorney received an organized inventory instead of a box of papers.
  • What the records showed was kept separate from what the client remembered.
  • A draft declaration narrative was ready for counsel to review.
  • A checklist showed which official records were still needed.

Preparation, not a court filing. Whether any record can be sealed or cleared is the attorney’s decision.

Safety & Documentation · 2026

A Safety Plan for Someone Being Stalked

A person experiencing stalking needed practical steps to feel safer at home and a way to keep a usable record of what was happening.

  • A prioritized home-security plan, starting with a locksmith assessment and door and window reinforcement.
  • Camera guidance built around evidence: recorded video with timestamps that can be exported and kept, not just live viewing.
  • Digital-safety steps and secure storage for photos, messages and other evidence.
  • A clear emergency plan, including calling 911 rather than confronting anyone.

Safety planning is individual and changes as a situation changes. VAA doesn’t provide legal advice or security services.
